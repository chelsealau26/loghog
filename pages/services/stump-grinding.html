Why grind a stump

  • Stumps get in the way of mowing and landscaping, and they're easy to trip over.
  • Rotting stumps attract termites and other wood-boring insects.
  • Some trees keep sending up sprouts from the stump and roots.
  • You want to plant, lay sod or build where the tree used to be.

How it works

A stump grinder chews the stump and the top of the main roots into wood chips, down below the surface. What's left is a patch of chips and soil you can fill with topsoil and seed or sod.

Already have a stump from an old removal or a tree that fell in a storm? We grind stumps on their own too. One customer had a large stump from a fallen oak “cut up and grinded down flat in no time.”

What's included

  • Grinding below ground level
  • Surface roots near the stump ground down as well
  • Chips left as mulch or cleaned up, your choice (ask when you get your quote)
  • Several stumps? Send them all and we'll quote them together

Getting a quote

Text a photo of each stump with something for scale (a shoe or a tape measure works) and your address. Let us know if there's a gate and how wide it is.

Questions about stump grinding

How deep do you grind?

Below ground level, deep enough for grass or new plantings. If you plan to build or pour concrete over the spot, tell us and we'll talk about it.

What happens to the roots?

The stump and the top of the main roots get ground up. The rest of the roots stay in the ground and break down naturally over time.

Can you get a grinder through my gate?

Usually, yes. Measure your gate opening and send it with your photos so we can plan for it.
