Why trim your trees

  • Storm prep. Dead and weak limbs are the first things to come down in a summer thunderstorm or a hurricane. Taking them out ahead of time is cheaper than cleaning them off your roof.
  • Clearance. Branches rubbing the roof, hanging over the driveway or growing into lines.
  • Tree health. Removing dead, diseased and crossing branches lets light and air through the canopy.
  • Looks. A trimmed live oak or magnolia makes the whole yard look cared for.

How we trim

We climb the tree and make clean cuts at the right spots, so the tree can seal over the wounds and keep its natural shape. We don't “top” trees: cutting the top off a tree leaves weak regrowth and usually shortens its life.

When we're done, we haul away the branches and clean up the yard.

What's included

  • Dead wood removal and thinning
  • Roof, driveway and house clearance
  • Shaping for live oaks, magnolias, crepe myrtles and more
  • Bushes and shrubs trimmed while we're there
  • Cleanup and hauling of everything we cut

Questions about tree trimming & pruning

When is the best time to trim trees in Pensacola?

Most trees can be trimmed any time of year here. Many people schedule it in late winter or spring, before hurricane season starts in June. Dead or hanging limbs should come down as soon as you notice them.

How often should trees be trimmed?

Every few years is typical for most yard trees. Fast growers, and trees close to the house, may need it more often.

Do you top trees?

No. Topping leaves weak, fast regrowth that breaks easily and stresses the tree. We thin and reduce the canopy with proper cuts instead.
